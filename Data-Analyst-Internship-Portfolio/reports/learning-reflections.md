# Learning Reflections

Written from the evidence in my four project repositories.

**Good data work is mostly preparation.** Task 1 and the first half of Task 2 were about finding problems: missing ages and cities, a repeated Order_ID and a partial final month. None of these are exciting, but every later number depended on handling them openly.

**A chart is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.** In Task 3 the dashboard showed Bengaluru with the highest average order value and Gaya and Delhi with the lowest, and a dip in August and September. In Task 4 I tested those ideas. The city difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.505) and the Q3 dip was borderline (p = 0.081). This changed how I word recommendations: I now say what is proven and what is only directional.

**Not every test finds something, and that is useful.** Five of the six hypotheses were not rejected. That supports a practical decision, such as keeping national pricing uniform rather than creating city-specific prices. The one clear result, uneven demand across categories (Electronics had the most orders), is something a business can act on.

**Significance and importance are different.** Reporting effect sizes and confidence intervals next to p-values helped me avoid over-reading small differences.

**Limits matter.** The dataset covers one year, most customers appear once and some fields are evenly distributed. Writing these limits down made the work more honest.

**What I want to improve.** I want to work with multi-year data, study retention and try customer segmentation methods such as RFM, which I listed as future work in Task 2.
